# Concept Validation Scorecard

This scorecard ranks all five Dew Monday concepts using the same 25-question interview framework from the Customer Research Guide. Each concept is scored across five dimensions — and the totals tell us which one to build first.

<div class="dm-pull-quote">"A scorcard doesn't make the decision. It makes the bias visible."</div>

The five dimensions match the five interview sections: **Problem Clarity**, **Market Demand**, **Creator Fit**, **Revenue Potential**, and **Execution Complexity** (inverted — lower complexity scores higher).

---

## Scoring Method

<div class="dm-resources">
  <div class="dm-resource">
    <div class="dm-resource-num">1</div>
    <div class="dm-resource-content">
      <h4>Problem Clarity (0–20)</h4>
      <p>How clearly defined is the problem this concept solves? Is the pain point specific, frequent, and felt by a real audience? High scores go to concepts where the customer's frustration is obvious and consistent across interviews.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="dm-resource">
    <div class="dm-resource-num">2</div>
    <div class="dm-resource-content">
      <h4>Market Demand (0–20)</h4>
      <p>Is there demonstrated willingness to pay? Do comparable products exist with real traction? High scores go to concepts where buyers are already spending money on adjacent solutions.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="dm-resource">
    <div class="dm-resource-num">3</div>
    <div class="dm-resource-content">
      <h4>Creator Fit (0–20)</h4>
      <p>How well does this concept match Dew Monday's identity, expertise, and editorial voice? High scores go to concepts where the brand adds genuine value — not just distribution.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="dm-resource">
    <div class="dm-resource-num">4</div>
    <div class="dm-resource-content">
      <h4>Revenue Potential (0–20)</h4>
      <p>What's the realistic path to $5K/month within 12 months? Scores consider unit economics, repeat purchase rate, and scalability without proportional cost increase.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="dm-resource">
    <div class="dm-resource-num">5</div>
    <div class="dm-resource-content">
      <h4>Execution Simplicity (0–20)</h4>
      <p>How much infrastructure, capital, and time does launch require? This is inverted — simpler execution scores higher. High scores go to concepts launchable with minimal upfront cost.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

## The Recommendation

<div class="dm-spotlight">
  <div class="dm-spotlight-label">✦ Build Order</div>
  <div class="dm-spotlight-name">Start with Creator Hub. Layer in Little Makers. Add Camera Drops at month 3.</div>
  <p class="dm-spotlight-body">Creator Hub is already live and already producing content. The gap between where it is and where it generates revenue is a monetization layer — a paid tier, a course, a sponsorship package — not a new product. That's a 4-week build, not a 6-month one.<br><br>Little Makers runs parallel because the content format (activities, guides, tool picks) shares infrastructure with Creator Hub and requires no inventory. Two concepts, one operational system.<br><br>Camera Drops comes third because it requires supplier relationships and drop logistics that take time to set up correctly. Rushing it produces a mediocre product. Building audience first with Creator Hub means Camera Drops launches to people who already trust the editorial voice.</p>
  <div class="dm-spotlight-quote">Art Drops and Tutorial App are Q3–Q4 conversations, not Q2 priorities.</div>
</div>
