# Customer Research Interview Guide

> Use these questions 1-on-1 with 3–5 people per concept. Aim for 20–30 minute conversations. Record with permission, or take raw notes. You're listening for language — not just answers. The words people use to describe their problems become your copy.

---

## How to Run These

**Before you start:**
- Pick one concept per session. Don't mix.
- Open with: *"There are no wrong answers — I'm trying to understand how you think about this, not sell you anything."*
- Let silence sit. The best answers come after a pause.

**During:**
- Follow the energy. If they go deep on something unexpected, go with them.
- Ask "why" one more time than feels comfortable.

**After:**
- Note exact phrases verbatim. Circle anything that surprises you.
- Look for: recurring pain points, words they repeat, moments of unexpected enthusiasm.

## 1. Camera Dropshipping — Curated Photography Gear

*Target: hobbyist photographers, amateur filmmakers, people who've Googled "best camera under $1000" in the past 6 months.*

**Q1 — Discovery & Trust**
*"When you're researching camera gear, where do you usually start — and what makes you actually trust a recommendation?"*
> Listen for: YouTube vs. Reddit vs. forums vs. Amazon reviews. Where does distrust come from?

**Q2 — Decision Friction**
*"Walk me through the last time you bought a piece of gear. What made it hard to decide, and what finally made you pull the trigger?"*
> Listen for: decision paralysis triggers, price anchoring, fear of regret.

**Q3 — The Overwhelm**
*"There are hundreds of options for almost any piece of gear. What do you wish someone would just tell you — clearly — so you didn't have to wade through all of it?"*
> Listen for: what kind of curation they actually want (budget range, use case, level?).

**Q4 — Price & Value**
*"Have you ever spent more on gear than you planned to? What convinced you it was worth it — or did you regret it?"*
> Listen for: what "worth it" means to them. Durability? Resale? Output quality?

**Q5 — The Gap**
*"Is there a piece of gear you've been eyeing for a while but haven't bought yet? What's stopping you?"*
> Listen for: the real blocker — is it money, uncertainty, or not knowing if it fits their use case?

## 2. Art Dropshipping — 72-Hour Limited Print Drops

*Target: people who follow artists on Instagram, have bought prints before, or describe themselves as "art lovers who don't collect art."*

**Q1 — Buying History**
*"Have you ever bought art — a print, poster, anything — for your space? What made you buy that specific thing?"*
> Listen for: emotional triggers (the artist's story, the image itself, the moment), and what "art" means to them vs. "decor."

**Q2 — Discovery**
*"How do you find artists or artwork you like? Walk me through how that usually happens."*
> Listen for: Instagram vs. Etsy vs. Pinterest vs. galleries. What converts browsing into buying intent?

**Q3 — The Scarcity Question**
*"If you saw a print you loved but it was only available for 72 hours, how would that make you feel? Would you buy it faster, or does that kind of thing stress you out?"*
> Listen for: genuine urgency response vs. anxiety. Not everyone likes FOMO mechanics.

**Q4 — The Friction**
*"What's stopped you from buying more art in the past — assuming you've seen things you liked?"*
> Listen for: price ceiling, uncertainty about quality, not knowing how it'll look IRL, shipping anxiety.

**Q5 — The Artist Relationship**
*"Does knowing the story behind a piece of art change how you feel about buying it? Can you give me an example?"*
> Listen for: whether provenance and narrative actually move the needle, or if it's noise.

## 3. Art Tutorial App — Project-First Creative Education

*Target: adults who want to learn to draw/paint, have tried YouTube tutorials, or bought a course they never finished.*

**Q1 — The Failed Attempt**
*"Have you ever tried to learn to draw, paint, or make art on your own? What happened?"*
> Listen for: where they gave up (the "I just can't" moment), and what triggered the attempt in the first place.

**Q2 — The YouTube Problem**
*"If you've used YouTube to learn something creative, what was good about it? What was frustrating?"*
> Listen for: the rabbit hole problem, no feedback loop, no sense of progress, skipping around.

**Q3 — What Progress Feels Like**
*"When you imagine actually getting better at making art — what does that look like to you? How would you know you'd improved?"*
> Listen for: their mental model of skill development. Is it output-based ("I can draw faces"), time-based, or feeling-based?

**Q4 — Project vs. Skill**
*"Would you rather learn 'how to shade' as a standalone lesson, or learn shading because you're working on a specific drawing? Does the order matter to you?"*
> Listen for: validation of project-first learning vs. traditional skill-tree approach.

**Q5 — Commitment & Consistency**
*"What time of day do you think you'd actually sit down and do creative work? And what would make you skip it?"*
> Listen for: real life constraints — the "when life gets busy" moment. What's realistic vs. aspirational?

## 4. Creator Hub — Monday Newsletter + Community

*Target: independent creators, freelancers, small business owners who make things — photography, writing, design, crafts, video.*

**Q1 — Information Diet**
*"What newsletters or content do you actually read every week? What makes you open them vs. let them pile up?"*
> Listen for: subject line triggers, sender trust, length tolerance, the "I feel smarter after reading this" quality.

**Q2 — The Monday Frame**
*"How do you usually feel on Monday mornings? Do you have any rituals or routines that help you start the week?"*
> Listen for: whether Monday feels like a reset or a dread. Does a "Monday newsletter" framing resonate or feel like pressure?

**Q3 — Creator Pain Points**
*"What's the hardest part of sustaining creative work — not the making, but everything around it?"*
> Listen for: isolation, business confusion, inconsistency, not knowing what to work on, income anxiety.

**Q4 — Community**
*"Are you part of any creator communities — Discord, Slack, subreddits, anything? What's worked? What's exhausted you?"*
> Listen for: community fatigue, the signal-to-noise problem, whether they want peers or mentors or just lurking.

**Q5 — Value Exchange**
*"If a newsletter consistently gave you one thing you could actually use each week — a tool, a resource, a mindset shift — would you pay for it? What would that be worth to you?"*
> Listen for: price sensitivity, what "useful" means to them, and whether they have a reference point (other paid newsletters they subscribe to).

## 5. Little Makers — Free Kids Creativity Hub

*Target: parents, caregivers, grandparents of children ages 0–8. Especially people who want to encourage creativity without buying a lot of stuff.*

**Q1 — Current Creative Routine**
*"What kinds of creative activities do your kids do right now — at home, at school, anywhere? What do they love? What do they resist?"*
> Listen for: what "creative activity" means to them (is it art, building, imaginative play?), and what they're already doing vs. what feels aspirational.

**Q2 — The Resource Search**
*"When you're looking for activity ideas for your kids, where do you go? What's frustrating about that search?"*
> Listen for: Pinterest overwhelm, recipe-style activity posts that require too many supplies, age-range confusion, too much screen time required.

**Q3 — The Supplies Problem**
*"Have you ever seen a great activity idea and then given up because you didn't have the supplies? What happened?"*
> Listen for: household-material workarounds they already use, frustration with "craft store haul" content, desire for simplicity.

**Q4 — Age & Development**
*"How do you think about what's 'right' for your child's age when it comes to creative activities? How do you figure out what they're ready for?"*
> Listen for: whether developmental framing matters to them, or if they just follow what the kid is interested in.

**Q5 — The No-Paywall Test**
*"If you found a site with really good, honestly free activity ideas — no email required, no account, no upsell — would that feel surprising? Would you trust it?"*
> Listen for: digital skepticism, what builds trust for a free resource, whether they'd share it and with whom.
