SPOTLIGHT 001 JUNE 2026 INDEPENDENT ART

Solitude Series — Mara Voss

Six pieces. One body of work. Three years in a converted sun porch in Minneapolis.

"I've been making work about being alone in rooms for eight years. It took me until last year to realize that's the work."
✦ Artist Profile
Mara Voss
@maravoss.studio · Minneapolis, MN · 6,200 followers

Works out of a converted sun porch with north-facing light she describes as "the only good thing about Minnesota winters." Day job in graphic design. Has been making personal work since 2018 — oil pastel first, then digital integration starting in 2022 when a broken wrist meant she couldn't hold a pastel stick for four months and had to figure out another way.

The Solitude Series started as a pandemic project and became the body of work she's most proud of. Six pieces made over three years. Each one is a different room. Each room has one person, or the feeling of one person who just left.

"The rooms aren't sad. They're just honest about what it feels like to be the only one in them."

The Work

Mara works in oil pastel on toned paper, then digitizes and adjusts in Procreate before sending to print. The result sits somewhere between illustration and painting — warm, slightly grainy, unmistakably human. She sells originals once a year at a small show in her city. The rest of the year, she posts on Instagram to 6,200 people who keep asking where they can buy prints.

🪟
Morning Window

A figure at a kitchen window, coffee cup, morning light coming in sideways. The most requested piece from her Instagram. Her most recognizable work — the one people screenshot and ask about in the comments.

Oil pastel + digital 11×14 in Giclée print
📚
Shelf Life

A bookshelf with objects arranged by someone who lives alone. A mug used as a pen holder. Shoes by the door. The kind of specific detail that makes you recognize a life.

Oil pastel + digital 9×12 in Giclée print
🍳
Kitchen at Night

Single overhead light, dishes in the sink, chair pulled away from a table. The most "lonely" piece in the series — Mara says it's actually the most peaceful.

Oil pastel + digital 11×14 in Giclée print
🪞
Bathroom Mirror

A reflection of a figure who could be anyone. Toothbrush in a cup. Towel on a hook. The face just out of frame enough to be yours.

Oil pastel + digital 8×10 in Giclée print
🛏️
Unmade Bed

Warm tones. Sheets twisted. Pillow with the indent of a head still in it. Her personal favorite. The one she says she would keep if she could only keep one.

Oil pastel + digital 11×14 in Giclée print
🚪
Door, Slightly Open

Hallway light coming through a bedroom door cracked two inches. The final piece in the series, made last. The one that closes it without closing it.

Oil pastel + digital 9×12 in Giclée print

Why this artist

She's been making work for eight years without a big following. The work is good enough to sustain attention — the kind of art that people save and come back to — but she has no distribution strategy beyond Instagram. She sells originals once a year locally and hasn't figured out print fulfillment at scale.

That's exactly who Art Drops exists for. Someone making real work who just needs more people to see it.

"The rooms aren't sad. They're just honest about what it feels like to be the only one in them."

What I'd change

Renny's Editorial — First Person
The infrastructure is what's missing. The work is already there.
  • She needs a proper print shop. Not Etsy — something like Big Cartel or Shopify with a good print-on-demand integration. The friction of finding her work is real.
  • Her Instagram bio doesn't mention prints are available. People literally ask in the comments and she responds individually. That's not a distribution strategy, that's exhaustion.
  • A simple email list of 200 people would probably sell out a print run in hours. She doesn't have one.
  • The work is strong enough to carry an audience. The infrastructure is what's missing — and that's fixable.
