# The Business of Making

At some point, every creator runs into the same wall.

You've made the thing. People seem to like the thing. Someone says, *"You should sell this."* And then a very specific kind of dread sets in — the dread of pricing, of invoices, of turning something you love into something you might hate.

I've watched talented people let this moment stop them completely. They keep the work free, or they underprice it so dramatically that the economics never work, or they overprice from fear and then nothing sells and they take it as confirmation that they weren't cut out for this anyway.

<div class="dm-pull-quote">"The business of making isn't separate from the creative work. It's just another craft to learn."</div>

You can be bad at it when you start. You get better with practice, the same as anything else.

The goal this week isn't to have a perfect pricing strategy. It's to name a number, put it in public, and see what happens. Most of the fear is about the unknown. A real price — even a wrong one — gives you data.

## Creator Spotlight

<div class="dm-spotlight">
  <div class="dm-spotlight-label">✦ This Week's Maker</div>
  <div class="dm-spotlight-name">Marcus Bell — Freelance Brand Designer</div>
  <p class="dm-spotlight-body">Marcus spent three years doing spec work and logo contests before he raised his rates so high he was sure no one would hire him. His first client at the new rate booked within a week. He now does eight brand projects a year, maximum. He turns away more work than he takes — clients include a regional restaurant group, a sustainable outdoor apparel brand, and two independent record labels. He books projects six months in advance and has never run a promotion.</p>
  <div class="dm-spotlight-quote">"I used to think the low prices were how I competed. Then I realized the low prices were why clients didn't take me seriously."</div>

## One Prompt

<div class="dm-prompt-box">
  <div class="dm-prompt-label">This Week's Question</div>
  <div class="dm-prompt-text">Name your price. Pick one thing you make — one skill, one product, one service — and write down what you would charge for it if you were sure it would sell. Don't undercut that number. Just write it somewhere real.</div>
