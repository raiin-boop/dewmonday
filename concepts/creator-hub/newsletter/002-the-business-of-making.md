# Issue #002 — The Business of Making

**Monday, June 23, 2026 · Business**

---

## Opening Thought

At some point, every creator runs into the same wall.

You've made the thing. People seem to like the thing. Someone says, *"You should sell this."* And then a very specific kind of dread sets in — the dread of pricing, of invoices, of turning something you love into something you might hate.

I've watched talented people let this moment stop them completely. They keep the work free, or they underprice it so dramatically that the economics never work, or they overprice from fear and then nothing sells and they take it as confirmation that they weren't cut out for this anyway.

Here's what I've come to believe: the business of making isn't separate from the creative work. It's just another craft to learn. You can be bad at it when you start. You get better with practice, the same as anything else.

The goal this week isn't to have a perfect pricing strategy. It's to name a number, put it in public, and see what happens. Most of the fear is about the unknown. A real price — even a wrong one — gives you data.

## Three Resources

**1. "The Business of Belonging" — David Spinks**
Not a marketing book. A community-building book — which, for independent creators, is the same thing. The central idea is that people buy from creators they feel connected to, and community is how you scale that connection beyond 1:1 relationships. Dense with frameworks, light on jargon.

**2. Gumroad (zero monthly fee)**
For selling digital products — presets, templates, e-books, printables, patterns — Gumroad takes a flat 10% per transaction and nothing else. No monthly subscription, no minimum sales. The discovery features are limited, but for getting your first product live today, there's nothing faster.

**3. The "1,000 True Fans" Essay — Kevin Kelly**
Written in 2008 and still the clearest framing of sustainable independent creative income I've found. The math is simple: 1,000 people willing to spend $100/year on your work = $100,000/year. You probably don't need a million followers. You need a thousand real ones.

## The Work

**Creator Spotlight: Marcus Bell, Freelance Brand Designer**

Marcus spent three years doing spec work and logo contests before he raised his rates so high he was sure no one would hire him. His first client at the new rate booked within a week.

He now does eight brand projects a year, maximum. He turns away more work than he takes. His client roster includes a regional restaurant group, a sustainable outdoor apparel brand, and two independent record labels.

*"I used to think the low prices were how I competed. Then I realized the low prices were why clients didn't take me seriously."*

He books projects six months in advance and has never run a promotion.

---

## One Prompt

**Name your price.**

Pick one thing you make — one skill, one product, one service — and write down what you would charge for it if you were sure it would sell. Don't undercut that number. Just write it. You don't have to publish it today. But write it down somewhere real.
