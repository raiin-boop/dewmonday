# Issue #004 — The Myth of the Perfect Setup

**Monday, July 7, 2026 · Gear**

---

## Opening Thought

Somewhere, right now, a creator is not making anything because they're waiting on a lens. Or a desk upgrade. Or the new MacBook. Or the right editing software. Or until the lighting situation in their room is sorted.

I know this creator. I've been this creator.

There's a specific flavor of procrastination that disguises itself as preparation. It feels like due diligence — after all, better tools make better work, don't they? Sometimes. Marginally. But only if you're already making work. If you're not making anything, better tools won't fix that. They'll give you something new to optimize while the real problem — the blank page, the vulnerability of putting something out — stays untouched.

Here's the uncomfortable gear truth: the setup that professional creators use is the result of years of making things with worse setups. The nice camera came after the grainy phone videos. The studio desk came after the kitchen table. The fancy mic came after the built-in laptop audio. The progression only makes sense in that order.

The alternative is to keep upgrading without ever publishing, which is comfortable and completely unproductive.

This week's issue is about gear — but it's really about the habit of making with what you have. The tools will follow the work. That's how it's always worked.

## The Gear Take

**Godox SL60W** ($90)

Lighting is the single most underrated upgrade in creator setups, and the Godox SL60W is the most value for money light I know of at any budget level. It's a 60-watt LED bowens-mount monolight — which means it works with a huge range of modifiers (softboxes, umbrellas, reflectors) that you can pick up cheaply.

The light itself is daylight-balanced at 5600K, dimmable to about 10%, and quiet enough for video use. One SL60W with a medium softbox can transform a home recording setup from "obviously a bedroom" to "intentional and professional."

It doesn't require a camera. Point it at your face on a Zoom call, a podcast recording, a TikTok. The ROI on this $90 is higher than almost any camera upgrade you could make.

## Three Resources

**1. MKBHD's "The Best Camera" video**
Marques Brownlee made a video years ago arguing that the best camera is the one you have with you. It holds up. He also walks through how much of the "professional" quality in his own videos has nothing to do with the camera and everything to do with light, audio, and consistency. Good 20-minute watch for any creator who films anything.

**2. Notion (free tier)**
Not a gear pick, but a setup pick. Notion is the closest thing to a universal creative OS I've found — and the free tier handles everything a solo creator needs: project tracking, content calendars, idea capture, link databases, mood boards. The learning curve is real but the payoff is a setup that actually fits how you think, not how someone else says you should think.

**3. Creative Cloud Photography Plan** ($10/month)
If you're doing any photo editing and you're not on Lightroom yet — this is still the benchmark. The Photography plan includes Lightroom and Photoshop for $10/month. Lightroom's preset system means you can develop a consistent visual style quickly, which matters for brand coherence across all the content you make.

## The Work

**Creator Spotlight: Jerome Walsh, @jeromemakesthings**

Jerome makes furniture. His workshop is a one-car garage with a $200 workbench he built himself, a used bandsaw, and a set of hand tools he's accumulated over eight years. He shoots all his content on an iPhone 13 mounted on a $25 tripod.

His YouTube channel has 180,000 subscribers. His online plans and templates bring in a consistent five figures monthly.

He's been offered woodworking tool sponsorships worth more than his current annual income. He's turned most of them down. *"If I can't use it honestly, I can't talk about it honestly. That's the whole thing."*

He built his first piece of furniture at a community college continuing-ed class with borrowed tools.

## One Prompt

**What's one piece of gear you've been waiting to buy before you start something?**

Write it down. Then write down what you'd make if you had it. Now make a version of that thing with what you have today. Not a perfect version. Just a version. See what actually holds you back — I'd bet it isn't the gear.
